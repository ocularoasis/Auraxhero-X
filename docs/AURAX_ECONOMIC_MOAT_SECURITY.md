# AuraX Economic Moat, Opportunity Protection, and Security

## Purpose

AuraX is an economic opportunity network. An opportunity is a protected economic object, not a disposable lead card. The platform must create measurable value, route it to an eligible human or authorized agent, preserve attribution, facilitate execution, and capture legitimate platform economics.

## Economic spine

`MARKET SIGNAL → DEMAND → OPPORTUNITY → QUALIFICATION → ROUTING → CLAIM → PROTECTED DISCLOSURE → WORK → DEAL → TRANSACTION → ATTRIBUTION → COMMISSION → AURAX REVENUE → OUTCOME → LEARNING`

No production UI may display invented deal values, commissions, savings, transaction volume, earnings, or conversion rates. Every economic number must resolve to a production record and an auditable calculation.

## Opportunity taxonomy

Suggested top-level taxonomy:

The taxonomy is data-driven. A field page is indexable only when it has real published content/opportunities or legitimate informational content; empty categories must render an honest empty state rather than fabricated inventory.

## Opportunity protection

### Progressive disclosure

Public discovery exposes only what is necessary to establish relevance. Sensitive identity/contact/deal details are withheld until the participant is authenticated, eligible, and authorized to receive them.

### Claim and commitment

A participant may claim an opportunity only after the platform records the opportunity terms, eligibility, attribution rules, expiration/claim window, and applicable commercial arrangement. High-value or high-risk opportunities may require additional verification or a refundable commitment mechanism where legally and commercially appropriate.

### Attribution

Every opportunity has a durable identifier. The system records origin, qualification, routing, claim, disclosure, activity, introduction, deal state, transaction state, and economic settlement. Attribution is evidence, not a promise that compensation is legally owed; applicable commercial terms must be established before protected disclosure or compensated activity.

### Anti-circumvention

Where appropriate and legally enforceable, opportunity terms may prohibit circumvention and misuse. The product must prefer positive economic incentives for staying on-platform: continuous opportunity flow, verified reputation, deal tooling, attribution, commission accounting, and access to future opportunities.

## Opportunity continuity becomes a protection layer

Opportunity continuity is not only a lead-replenishment feature. It is also an eligibility system.

A participant who materially breaches platform rules, abuses protected information, falsifies activity, commits attribution abuse, or causes a verified deal-integrity event can have opportunity access restricted, suspended, or revoked according to a documented policy.

Controls:

1. Freeze new opportunity claims while an integrity event is reviewed.
2. Preserve existing records and evidence; do not silently erase history.
3. Apply proportionate restrictions based on verified event severity.
4. Provide review/appeal paths where appropriate.
5. Restore access only after the required conditions are satisfied.
6. Repeated or coordinated abuse can trigger permanent exclusion under the platform's terms and applicable law.

### Account-abuse resistance

Do not rely on email uniqueness alone. The security boundary should support risk signals such as verified identity, trusted device/session signals, credential integrity, account history, payout identity, organization relationships, and abuse patterns. Do not use opaque behavioral guesses as sole grounds for permanent exclusion. Security decisions must be auditable and policy-bound.

## Institutional-grade security target

The target is a high-assurance control plane comparable in design discipline to financial platforms, without claiming certification or parity with any specific company.

Required controls include:

- phishing-resistant MFA/passkeys where supported
- session and device risk controls
- step-up authentication for sensitive actions
- least-privilege authorization
- immutable/auditable security events
- encryption in transit and at rest
- secrets isolation and rotation
- payout/change-of-destination protections
- anomaly detection and rate limits
- transaction and commission idempotency
- privileged-action approval controls
- recovery procedures that resist account takeover
- incident response and evidence preservation
- separation of public discovery from protected economic data

No client-side role text, URL, local storage value, AI inference, or hidden UI state is an authorization source.

## Human + AuraX economics

The participant is treated as a commissioned economic operator where the business model supports it:

`AURAX ORIGINATES/QUALIFIES → HUMAN WORKS → TRANSACTION CLOSES → CONTRACTED COMMISSION EVENT → HUMAN SHARE + AURAX SHARE`

The split is a configured commercial term, not a hard-coded universal percentage. Revenue is recognized only from real, settled, attributable economic events.

## Executive economic hunter

Executive automation can search authorized data for incremental value:

- new demand
- new supply
- cross-sell opportunities
- referral opportunities
- savings opportunities
- unused capacity
- market movements
- machine-commerce opportunities
- partner opportunities

Bots may surface and route opportunities, but authorization boundaries, commercial terms, and protected-data access remain explicit.

The Executive OS should expose economic leakage and ROI evidence, including failed deals, abandoned claims, attribution disputes, abuse patterns, and cost-to-serve.

## Cyber-risk / red-team intelligence

AuraX may support a separate, tightly governed cyber-risk intelligence capability that monitors abuse and malicious activity relevant to platform safety and, where lawful and contractually permitted, produces vetted threat intelligence for cybersecurity providers.

This must **not** become a marketplace for raw personal information or a system for indiscriminate surveillance.

Required safeguards:

- collect only data necessary for a defined security purpose
- prefer indicators, techniques, infrastructure relationships, hashes, domains, URLs, attack patterns, and other defensible threat-intelligence artifacts over raw personal data
- minimize and redact personal information where possible
- maintain provenance and confidence for every intelligence item
- distinguish observation from inference
- establish retention and deletion rules
- enforce access controls and customer-specific entitlements
- maintain legal basis and contractual purpose for collection/use/disclosure
- provide review and correction mechanisms where appropriate
- prohibit retaliation, doxxing, or unauthorized disclosure
- never sell raw PII as a security product
- route high-risk intelligence decisions through authorized human review

The commercial product is **vetted threat intelligence**, not a database of people to expose.
